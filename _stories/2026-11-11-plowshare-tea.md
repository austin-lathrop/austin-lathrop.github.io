Every night, Steven and Vickie Coker brew a batch of tea made from roselle, lemon verbena and apple mint they grow themselves near Smithonia.

“Everybody in here can be a tea-making expert to some degree, anyway,” Vickie told members of Plowshare on Monday night. “It's a trial and error thing.”

About 40 people attended the farm and garden group’s November meeting at the Farm Bureau on Monday night. Rather than the usual guest speaker, members discussed how they grow, dry and brew their own teas after a potluck dinner, then held a tasting of members’ teas.

Even the Cokers’ nightly cup depends on the group. When Vickie’s roselle doesn’t grow well, fellow member Carol Williamson’s usually does, so the two trade.

That kind of community is why Plowshare exists. The group began in 2014 as a way for local gardeners to share knowledge, seeds and plants, said Steven, a founding member.

“We just have, as you can see, the sweetest camaraderie here amongst our members because we all love to play in the dirt,” he said.

Members sampled large batches of roselle tea, holy basil tea and a blend that Dee Pelewski makes from chamomile, calendula, marshmallow and elderflower. Most of the herbs were grown and dried by members and steeped loose in tea strainers.

The sharing doesn’t stop with tea.

Each spring, members fill tables with plants to give away, and through the summer, they pass along extra produce.

“You can never use all your stuff, you know, when it's coming in,” Steven said.

Members range from farmers to flower enthusiasts. Steven, 72, grew up on a farm and said he and Vickie have grown most of what they eat for the last 43 years.

The night ended with raffle prizes members brought to share: seeds, plants, T-shirts and other garden items.

Next month, the group will host a market.

“In December we have a marketplace, and so everybody brings the different products that they grow and stuff, and they sell them,” Steven said.
